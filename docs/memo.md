### Executive summary
**49 of 207 eligible tickets breached the first-response SLA (23.67%), representing ₹17,150 in estimated SLA-credit exposure.** Chat had the largest channel breach count; Morning-rostered resolving agents were attributed 41 breaches. These are review priorities, not evidence of individual fault. Exposure comprises ₹15,750 for completed tickets and ₹1,400 for open/pending tickets; it is not confirmed credits paid.

Against the complete week of 15–21 June, the breach rate moved from **23.59% to 23.67% (+0.08 percentage points)**. Breaches increased from **46 to 49**, while tickets increased from **195 to 207**. The rate was broadly unchanged; the higher count should be read alongside the higher volume.

### Operational observation
**34 of the 49 breaches (69.39%) arrived during Night hours and were attributed to Morning-rostered resolving agents.** Overnight queue carryover is worth investigating. Arrival time and resolving-agent attribution are different: this pattern does not establish attendance, staffing causation or individual responsibility.

### What to investigate next
- Review overnight queue handoffs and the 34 Night-arriving, Morning-attributed breaches; obtain queue and attendance records before drawing coverage conclusions.
- Inspect chat delays and Logistics routing with the relevant leads; review the two agents' actual cases before individual conversations.
- Track weekly progress against a **proposed 15% operating target—analyst-selected, not client-provided**. Review routing and handoffs within existing staffing; headcount is frozen until Q4.
